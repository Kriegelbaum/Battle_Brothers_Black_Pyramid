Warhorses: changelog, versions 0.1.3 – 0.4.1

Original mod · mod author: krigun

Since 0.4.0 horses are simpler: they have no perks, the Riding tree gives mounting, defense, horseback archery and Charge, and a rider is thrown off by the weapon that hits him — billhooks, halberds and pikes most of all. In the field a rider is faster than a man on foot, but rides at most 6 tiles a turn.

A horse is a roleplay choice for a fighter, not a free bonus. Horses differ in hitpoints, defense, attack and armor, like war dogs. A rider is faster than a man on foot on any terrain, but hits land on him more often (75%) than on the horse. A billhook, a halberd or a hook can throw him off, a shield is useless in the saddle, an archer shoots worse. There are reasons enough to dismount.

All changes are listed below.

0.4.1 — shields in the saddle, Charge push-back
♘
A shield is useless on horsebackReworked0.4.1
WasOnly Shieldwall and Knock Back were blocked on horseback; the shield gave its full defense.
NowOn horseback a shield gives no Melee or Ranged Defense and none of its skills can be used (Shieldwall, Knock Back, Reforged Cover Ally and any other shield skill). Shield perks (Phalanx, Rebuke and the like) act as if there were no shield. Its weight stays. Stated in the Riding description and in the shield and skill tooltips.
Why

A rider at the gallop does not hold a shield like a man in the line.

♘
Charge push-backReworked0.4.1
WasThe push-back chance was a horse stat (30–40% plus growth from stats).
NowChance = weapon + rider's armor weight + run-up − target's armor weight, from 0 to 90%. Weapon: two-handed spears and pikes 35%, two-handed hammers and maces 30%, polearms and one-handed spears 25%, maces, flails, two-handed axes 20%, two-handed swords and staffs 15%, axes and cleavers 12%, swords 10%, daggers 3%, whips 0. Rider's armor weight (helmet + body): 15–29 +5%, 30–44 +10%, 45+ +15%. Run-up: +3% per tile beyond two. Target: −1% per 3 weight of its armor. The horse adds nothing. The roll goes to the combat log. Per-weapon values are in the sheet, column F.
Why

The mass of horse and rider at speed does the pushing; the weapon decides how much of it reaches the target.

♘
New iconsNew0.4.1
WasThe horse skills and perks used old and vanilla icons.
NowMount, Dismount, Charge and the Riding, Advanced Riding, Horseback Archery and Charge perks have their own icons.
♘
Horse breakthrough removedReworked0.4.1
WasA rider hit by a free attack while leaving a Zone of Control kept moving with the horse's breakthrough chance (30–40% and more). The same chance pushed the target back on a Charge.
NowNo breakthrough any more: after a free attack the rider stops, like a man on foot. The breakthrough line is gone from the horse tooltips. Charge push-back is set by the weapon and armor weight (above).
Why

Riding away from enemies is harder. Horses differ like war dogs do: hitpoints, defense, attack and armor, not special traits.

♘
Dismounting and trapsReworked0.4.1
WasOn dismount the horse appeared on a nearby tile and the rider stayed put. Roots, nets and the cultist mancatcher net stayed on the rider, undrawn; the horse was free. No dismounting next to an enemy.
NowThe horse stays on its tile, the rider steps to the chosen free tile next to it. Shaman roots, nets, webs and the cultist mancatcher net (Davkul) stay on the horse together with Break Free; the rider is free. The same on a fall. Dismounting is allowed in an enemy Zone of Control, but an enemy with a wall of spears next to the chosen tile strikes first, and a hit keeps the rider in the saddle. No free tile, or held by a kraken tentacle: no dismounting. Mounting next to an enemy is still impossible.
♘
Hits go to the rider more oftenReworked0.4.1
WasA hit on a rider went to the horse with a chance equal to the horse’s “size” (50–60%).
NowA hit goes to the rider with a 75% chance and to the horse with 25%. Nothing changes it: not the horse, the weapon or perks. The rider tooltip says so.
Why

Enemies fight the rider, not the horse: the horse is equipment, the man does the fighting.

0.4.0 — Simple Horses, an Honest Tree
♘
Horses without perksReworked0.4.0
BeforeHorses rolled perks (Pathfinder, Colossus, Battle Forged, Relentless, Brawny, Dodge) in shops, on enemies and as loot; 20% of horses were also “steady” against falls.
NowHorses have no perks anywhere, and no steadiness either. Old horses from a save lose their perks on load. A horse is gear, not a second character.
Why

A simpler riding tree and a more even overall balance.

♘
Riding — mounting onlyReworked0.4.0
BeforeRiding gave mounting and the Charge skill.
NowRiding only lets the character mount a horse. The rules of riding stay: +3 Reach, 35% rider defense, the ranged penalty, hits that may go to the horse (25% since 0.4.1). The character's own perks work in the saddle, except those that need firm ground (Shieldwall, Spearwall, Riposte, Footwork, Lunge and the like).
♘
Advanced Riding — defense onlyReworked0.4.0
BeforeGave 85% defense, +10% of the horse's Initiative, +10 breakthrough and a lower fall chance by Stamina.
NowOnly 85% of the rider's own defense instead of 35%.
♘
Charge Training removedReworked0.4.0
BeforeThe perk gave the horse +40 Initiative and the Charge +10 breakthrough.
NowThe perk is gone. Whoever took it gets the perk point back, and it is removed from old perk trees.
♘
Deadly Charge → ChargeReworked0.4.0
BeforeEvery rider had Charge: two-handed weapons only, 2–3 tiles, a damage bonus for the run-up; Deadly Charge added +10% damage, a 50% stun, a morale check on neighbours and +10 Initiative.
NowOnly the Charge perk (former Deadly Charge) gives the Charge skill, 6 AP. The strike is made with the weapon in the main hand, any melee weapon, with no extra damage. 40% stun (not those immune). Fatigue: the weapon's attack plus the Fatigue of riding each tile of the run-up. A straight run-up from 2 tiles to what is left of the turn on horseback. On a hit, enemies next to the rider check morale. Once per turn, not while engaged. Push-back is set by the weapon and armor weight (0.4.1). All mounted enemies have Charge.
♘
Moving on horsebackReworked0.4.0
BeforeA horse table of its own: road and field 1 AP, forest 4, swamp 5 — 1 AP worse than on foot; a horse with Pathfinder did better. No limit to tiles per turn.
NowThe same terrain as on foot, 1 AP cheaper but at least 1: road and field 1, forest and rough ground 2, rocks, swamp and shallow water 3. It is worked out from the character's own cost on foot, so his Pathfinder works in the saddle. At most 6 tiles on horseback per turn, the Charge run-up included. Fatigue is half that on foot.
♘
Thrown off by the weaponReworked0.4.0
BeforeThe chance came from the Hitpoints damage (1% per 2% of Hitpoints) times a weapon factor, plus special-blow bonuses, minus horse steadiness and Advanced Riding.
NowEvery hit on the rider (not on the horse) rolls a chance that depends only on the weapon: from 20% for billhooks, halberds and voulges down to 2% for knives; arrows, bolts and thrown weapons 5%; no weapon and beasts 1%. The Hook skill adds +20% to the weapon's chance. Named weapons count as their plain version.
Why

Cavalry now has its proper counters — billhooks, halberds, pikes.

♘
Riderless horse: ReachNew0.4.0
BeforeA horse on the field without a rider had no Reach of its own.
NowA riderless horse has 3 Reach.
Rider: Initiative, Defense, Armor Weight
♘
Rider InitiativeReworked0.1.4
BeforeWhen mounted, a fighter took the horse’s whole Initiative: 150 plus the riding perk bonuses. His own Initiative was ignored, so a rider had no reason to train it.
NowThe rider keeps his own Initiative: base, armor weight, fatigue, his perks and effects. He gets no Initiative from the horse.
♘
Defense: Horse and Rider SeparatelyReworked0.1.4
BeforeThe rider’s defense was the horse’s defense (plus +30% of the fighter’s base defense with Advanced Riding). Sitting on a horse was a bad deal for a heavy knight because his defense was cut so hard.
NowBefore the hit roll, the game decides who takes the attack:
  • the horse — with a 25% chance (since 0.4.1 always, for any horse; before, the horse’s “size”, 50–60%); the roll is made against the horse’s defense and the horse takes the damage;
  • the rider — with a 75% chance, the roll is made against his own defense: 35% of it, 85% with Advanced Riding, with all bonuses included.

The hit chance preview shows an honest average: the defense weighted by the chance to hit the horse.

♘
Armor WeightReworked0.1.4
BeforeThe rider’s body armor and helmet weighed on the horse, lowering its Initiative and Stamina.
NowThe rider’s armor weighs on the rider himself, as it should. The horse carries only its own armor.
Riding Perk Tree
♘
RidingReworked0.1.7
BeforeA mounted rider’s Initiative was equal to the horse’s Initiative; the rider’s own Initiative was ignored.
NowA mounted rider’s Initiative is the rider’s own Initiative. The perk tooltip now says this directly and also explains falling off a horse and movement costs in the saddle.
♘
Horseback ArcheryReworked0.1.5 · 0.2.5
BeforeWithout the perk −30 Ranged Skill; the perk removed the penalty completely.
NowWithout the perk −35% Ranged Skill, with the perk −15%, and −10% with a crossbow. You can’t shoot from the saddle with no penalty at all — unless you are a Scythian warrior :)
Shooting from the Saddle and Height
♘
High Ground BonusNew0.1.5 · 0.1.6
BeforeThe rider used the normal height rules: +10% to hit from above, −10% per level when shooting uphill.
Now
  • shooting down — no bonus to hit, but range from high ground still increases;
  • shooting up — the horse’s height cancels one level of the penalty: one level up has no penalty, two levels −10%, three levels −20%.
Mounted Movement
♘
Fatigue in the SaddleReworked0.3.6
BeforeA rider paid the same Fatigue per tile as a man on foot: the horse carried him, but his legs still got tired.
NowMoving in the saddle costs half the Fatigue of moving on foot (rounded up, at least 1 per tile). It is counted from the brother’s own on-foot cost, so changes from Reforged and Hardened are kept. On foot again — normal cost.

♘
A dead horse stays deadFixed0.3.7
BeforeA horse killed on the field — your own after a fall or dismount, or an enemy one — could still turn up in the loot after the battle.
NowEvery horse that comes onto the field, yours or the enemy’s, is entered in a battle log, and its death is marked there. After the loot is gathered the log decides: dead — its item is not in the loot, wherever it came from; one of yours alive and on the field — its item is sure to be in the loot.
Why

A horse is a living fighter, not a thing: once killed, it must not come back.

Compatibility and Interface

Monk and HorseNew0.1.3

A brother with Monastery Training (Black Pyramid) cannot mount a horse. The two trees lock each other: taking Monastery Training closes the riding tree; taking Riding closes the monk tree. Enemy monks never appear on horseback.
